Inductors and Magnetic Fields

01 · MAGNETIC FIELDS

Current creates a magnetic field

An inductor is commonly a coil of wire. Current through the winding creates a magnetic field. A core can concentrate and shape that field. Inductance, measured in henries (H), describes how strongly the component opposes changes in current.

v = L × di/dt
02 · INDUCTANCE

Lenz's law and changing current

An inductor develops a voltage that opposes a change in its current. This is not opposition to current itself in the same way as a resistor. The effect depends on the rate of current change and the inductance: v = L × di/dt, with sign determined by the chosen voltage/current reference directions.

03 · ENERGY STORAGE

Energy in the magnetic field

For an ideal linear inductor, stored energy is E = ½LI². Energy rises with inductance and with the square of current. Real inductors have winding resistance, core losses, saturation limits and parasitic capacitance.

04 · RL TIME CONSTANT

Current rise and decay

In a simple series RL circuit, the time constant is τ = L/R. Following a DC step, current rises toward its steady-state value exponentially. When the source is removed, current still needs a path; abruptly interrupting an inductive current can create a high voltage.

05 · INDUCTOR FAMILIES

Air core, ferrite and iron core

Air-core coils avoid magnetic-core saturation but may need more turns. Ferrite cores are common at higher switching frequencies. Laminated iron cores are used in many lower-frequency magnetic components. Core material, geometry, winding and frequency determine behaviour.

06 · PRACTICAL USES

Filtering, conversion and actuation

Inductors appear in buck and boost converters, filters, chokes, transformers (as coupled inductors), motors, relays and solenoids. In switching converters, the inductor stores and transfers energy while current changes over each switching cycle.

07 · RATINGS AND SELECTION

Current, saturation and losses

Check inductance and tolerance, rated and saturation current, DC resistance, core material, operating frequency, temperature rise and insulation. Saturation reduces inductance and can cause current to rise sharply. Do not select by inductance value alone.

08 · SAFE LOW-VOLTAGE PRACTICE

Observe an RL response

Use a small inductor, a current-limiting resistor and an isolated extra-low-voltage DC supply. Measure current indirectly by measuring voltage across a known series resistor. Keep the circuit current within component ratings. Do not disconnect an energized coil without a designed current path.

09 · COMMON ERRORS

Do not treat an inductor as a resistor

An ohmmeter measures winding resistance, not inductance. An inductor does not block all DC after its transient has settled. A coil can generate a high voltage when current is interrupted. A flyback diode must be selected and connected with the correct polarity for the circuit.
