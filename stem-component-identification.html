01 · COMPONENT RECOGNITION

Start with what you can see

Appearance is a clue, not a guarantee. Packages vary between manufacturers, so read markings and verify unfamiliar parts.

BEGINNER
Fixed resistor

Limits current and helps set voltages. Many through-hole types use coloured bands; surface-mount types often use printed codes.

LED

A light-emitting diode. It is polarised and normally needs a current-limiting resistor in a simple indicator circuit.

Electrolytic capacitor

Stores electrical energy. Common aluminium electrolytic types are polarised; the sleeve stripe usually marks the negative lead.

Diode

Allows conventional current more readily in one direction. The body band on many axial diodes marks the cathode.

Potentiometer

A three-terminal adjustable resistor. The centre terminal is the wiper; it can act as a variable divider.

Transistor

Can act as an electronic switch or amplifier. Identify the exact part number and pinout before wiring; pin order is not universal.

02 · RESISTOR VALUES

Read the value before you use it

Resistor colour bands encode significant digits, a multiplier and tolerance.

Four-band resistor

Band 1 is the first digit, band 2 the second digit, band 3 the multiplier, and band 4 the tolerance.

Example: Brown · Black · Red · Gold = 10 × 100 Ω = 1,000 Ω (1 kΩ), with ±5% tolerance.

Five-band resistor

Band 1, 2 and 3 are the significant digits, band 4 is the multiplier, and band 5 is the tolerance.

Example: Brown · Black · Black · Brown · Brown = 100 × 10 Ω = 1,000 Ω (1 kΩ), with ±1% tolerance.

The tolerance is not an extra resistance value. A 1 kΩ resistor with ±5% tolerance may measure between 950 Ω and 1,050 Ω and still be within specification.

04 · POLARISED COMPONENTS

Recognise the marked terminal

Use the manufacturer's datasheet when markings or package conventions are unclear.

LED and ordinary diode

  • LED long lead is commonly the anode (+) on a new through-hole part.
  • Short lead is commonly the cathode (−).
  • A flat edge on many LED packages marks the cathode side.
  • The stripe on many axial rectifier diodes marks the cathode.

Electrolytic capacitor

  • The stripe on many aluminium electrolytic capacitors marks the negative terminal.
  • On many radial parts, the longer lead is positive, but do not rely on lead length alone.
  • Observe voltage and polarity ratings. Reversed or over-voltage operation can cause venting or rupture.
  • Discharge capacitors safely before handling or measuring a circuit.
