01 · WHY RESISTORS MATTER

One component, many jobs

A resistor does not simply “reduce voltage”. Its role depends on where it is connected and what the circuit needs.

FOUNDATION
01

Limit current

In series with an LED, a resistor helps set the current and protects the LED from excessive current under the intended operating conditions.

Example: LED series resistor
02

Divide voltage

Two resistors can create a lower voltage at their junction. The output changes when a load draws current, so divider loading matters.

Example: sensor signal scaling
03

Set logic states

Pull-up and pull-down resistors give an input a defined default HIGH or LOW state when a switch or open-collector output is not actively driving it.

Example: push-button input
04

Set operating points

Resistors can establish bias currents and voltages for transistor stages. The correct values depend on the device, topology and required operating point.

Example: transistor bias network

Important distinction: a resistor dissipates electrical energy as heat. It is not a voltage regulator. A divider may be suitable for a high-impedance signal input, but it is usually a poor substitute for a regulated power supply.

03 · RESISTOR POWER AND HEAT

Will the resistor run safely?

Resistance alone is not enough. A resistor also has a power rating, commonly expressed in watts.

Power in a resistor

For a resistor with voltage across it and current through it, power can be calculated using any of these equivalent relationships:

P = V × IP = I² × RP = V² ÷ R

Power is converted mainly into heat. If the resistor dissipates more than its permitted rating, it may overheat, drift in value, become damaged or fail.

Choose with margin

Do not select a resistor whose nominal rating is barely equal to the calculated dissipation. Consider ambient temperature, ventilation, enclosure, pulse conditions and the manufacturer's derating curve.

0.125 W · common small signal use0.25 W · common through-hole resistor0.5 W and above · higher dissipation where appropriate

These are common ratings, not automatic approvals for every circuit.

Heat is a design condition.

A resistor can become hot even when it is operating within its rating. Keep heat-producing components away from plastics, wires and heat-sensitive parts. Use the manufacturer's power-derating guidance.

04 · VOLTAGE DIVIDERS

Calculate the output, then add a load

A divider's output is taken across the lower resistor. A connected load sits in parallel with that lower resistor and changes the output voltage.

Ideal resistor model

Compare the unloaded output with the loaded output.

Unloaded output2.50 V
Loaded output2.50 V
Divider current, unloaded0.25 mA
Effective lower resistance with load10 kΩ
Vout = Vin × R2 ÷ (R1 + R2)

Practical rule: a divider is most predictable when the load resistance is much larger than the lower divider resistor. If the load is comparable to or smaller than R2, loading can significantly reduce the output. For powering a device, consider a suitable regulator instead.

05 · COMBINING RESISTORS

Series, parallel and standard values

Combine available resistors to obtain a useful equivalent value, but remember that each resistor still has its own power and tolerance limits.

Series combination

R1R2
Rtotal = R1 + R2

Resistances add. The same current flows through each resistor, while the supply voltage is divided between them.

Parallel combination

R1R2
1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2

The equivalent resistance is lower than the smallest branch resistance. Each branch has the same voltage.

Use the resistor colour-code calculator to identify a physical resistor's nominal value and tolerance. Standard E-series values mean the exact calculated resistance may not be available as a single component.

06 · POTENTIOMETERS

A resistor you can adjust

A potentiometer contains a resistive track and a movable wiper. Its three terminals can be used as an adjustable voltage divider, or two terminals can be used as a variable resistance.

END 1
END 2WIPER · ADJUSTABLE POSITION

Two common connections

  • Voltage divider: connect both ends of the track across a supply and take the variable output from the wiper.
  • Variable resistor: use the wiper and one end terminal. Some designs connect the wiper to the unused end for improved continuity if the wiper momentarily loses contact.

A potentiometer's total resistance and power rating must suit the circuit. Do not assume every potentiometer can carry motor or lamp current.

07 · PRACTICAL LOW-VOLTAGE BUILD

Build a current-limited LED circuit

Use a low-voltage DC source and compare the calculated current with a real measurement.

WORKBENCH ACTIVITY

Components and tools

  • Regulated 5 V DC supply or current-limited bench supply.
  • One standard LED with known forward-voltage information.
  • One 330 Ω resistor, preferably ¼ W or higher.
  • Breadboard and jumper wires.
  • Digital multimeter with intact probes.

Never connect this activity to mains electricity. Use a low-voltage, current-limited supply.

Build and investigate

  1. Identify the LED anode and cathode. Check the component datasheet if the markings are unclear.
  2. With power disconnected, connect the LED and 330 Ω resistor in series.
  3. Connect the series path to the 5 V supply with correct LED polarity.
  4. Check the wiring for shorts before applying power.
  5. Power the circuit and observe the LED. If it does not light, disconnect power before troubleshooting.
  6. Measure the voltage across the resistor with the meter in DC voltage mode.
  7. Calculate current using the measured resistor voltage divided by 330 Ω. Do not put the meter in current mode directly across the supply.
  8. Compare the measured current with the calculator estimate. Explain differences using LED forward voltage, resistor tolerance and supply variation.

08 · COMMON MISTAKES

What can go wrong?

LED connected without a current-limiting method

An LED connected directly to a stiff voltage source can draw excessive current and fail. Use a correctly designed driver or series resistor.

Resistor wattage ignored

A resistance value may be correct while the selected component's power rating is inadequate.

Divider used to power a load

A load changes the divider ratio. Use a regulator or suitable driver when the load needs a stable supply.

Pull-up or pull-down omitted

A digital input can float when nothing drives it. Provide a defined logic state using an appropriate resistor or a correctly configured internal pull-up.

Resistor measured in a live circuit

Switch off and safely discharge stored energy before measuring resistance. In-circuit parallel paths can also affect the reading.

Assuming colour bands are always obvious

Check band orientation, tolerance and the component's markings. Confirm uncertain values with a meter when safe and appropriate.
