01 · THE PN JUNCTION DIODE

A component with a preferred direction

A semiconductor diode is formed from a P-type region and an N-type region. Its two terminals are the anode and cathode.

FOUNDATION

Identify the terminals

ANODEP-type
CATHODEN-type

Conventional current can flow from anode to cathode when the diode is sufficiently forward-biased. The band on many axial diodes marks the cathode. Confirm the actual part marking and datasheet. If you need a refresher on identifying components and polarity markings, revisit Module 02: Component Identification and Polarity.

Read the circuit symbol

The triangle-like wedge in this simplified drawing points toward the cathode bar. In standard circuit notation, the diode symbol is a triangle-like filled arrowhead against a vertical bar.

Terminology: conventional current is described from positive to negative through the external circuit. Electron motion in a metal is in the opposite direction. Circuit diagrams normally use conventional current.

02 · FORWARD AND REVERSE BIAS

Polarity changes diode behaviour

Bias describes how an external voltage is applied across the diode junction.

FORWARD BIAS

Conducting direction

The anode is at a higher potential than the cathode. Once the junction is sufficiently forward-biased, current can flow. A silicon diode often has a forward drop around 0.6–0.7 V at some operating currents, but this is not a fixed universal value.

REVERSE BIAS

Blocking direction

The cathode is at a higher potential than the anode. The diode blocks most current, apart from a small leakage current. Exceeding the reverse-voltage rating can cause breakdown and damage unless the device is designed for that operating mode.

04 · LIGHT-EMITTING DIODES

An LED is a diode that emits light

LEDs convert electrical energy into light when forward-biased. Different colours and technologies have different forward-voltage characteristics.

LED polarity and current

The LED and resistor are in series. The resistor limits current according to the supply voltage, LED forward voltage and resistance. The LED itself does not reliably limit its own current.

Choose the resistor

For a simple series circuit:

R = (Vs − Vf) ÷ I

Example: a 5 V supply, LED forward voltage of 2 V and target current of 10 mA gives:

R = (5 − 2) ÷ 0.01 = 300 Ω

A nearby standard value such as 330 Ω gives approximately 9.1 mA under these assumptions. Confirm the LED's permitted current and forward-voltage range in its datasheet.

Open the LED resistor calculator

For multiple LEDs, the correct arrangement and resistor depend on the supply voltage, LED forward voltages, desired current and driver design. Do not assume one resistor per arbitrary group will always be suitable.

05 · RECTIFICATION

Using diodes to steer AC current

Rectifiers convert an alternating input into a unidirectional, pulsating output. A filter capacitor can smooth the output, but the result is not automatically a regulated DC supply.

Half-wave rectifier

One diode passes one half-cycle and blocks the other in the idealised model. The output contains large gaps and has substantial ripple.

Bridge rectifier

Four diodes steer both input half-cycles so load current keeps the same direction. Two diode drops are typically in the conducting path. This is a functional concept drawing, not a construction layout.

Safety boundary: this lesson does not provide a mains rectifier construction activity. Mains rectifiers and non-isolated power supplies can expose lethal voltage and stored energy. Study the concept using isolated, current-limited extra-low-voltage AC equipment under suitable supervision.

06 · PROTECTION AND REGULATION

Other useful diode applications

Diodes appear in many practical circuits beyond simple rectification.

Flyback diode

A relay or motor coil stores energy in its magnetic field. When the switch opens, the collapsing field can create a high voltage. A diode placed across a DC coil provides a path for the coil current to decay.

+VCOILSWDiode across coil, cathode to +V

For a typical low-side switched DC coil, the diode cathode connects to the positive supply and anode to the switched low side. Verify the circuit polarity and choose a diode rated for coil current and transient conditions.

Zener diode

A Zener diode is designed to operate in reverse breakdown within specified limits. With a suitable series resistor, it can provide a simple reference or limited shunt regulation in low-current applications.

It is not a universal replacement for a regulator. Check Zener voltage tolerance, test current, power dissipation, dynamic resistance and thermal conditions.

Schottky and fast diodes

Schottky diodes often have a lower forward voltage than standard silicon PN diodes, while fast-recovery diodes are designed for switching applications. Selection depends on switching speed, reverse voltage, current and losses.

A lower forward drop does not mean a part is suitable for every circuit. Always check the datasheet.

07 · SELECTING A DIODE

What should you check in a datasheet?

Do not select a diode by package appearance or one headline rating alone.

IFForward current

Permitted continuous forward current under stated conditions.

VRRMRepetitive peak reverse voltage

Maximum specified repetitive reverse-voltage rating.

VFForward voltage

Voltage drop at a stated forward current and temperature.

IFSMSurge forward current

Permitted non-repetitive surge under stated pulse conditions.

PDPower dissipation

Thermal limits depend on mounting, ambient temperature and package.

trrReverse recovery time

Important in switching circuits where the diode must turn off quickly.

08 · WORKBENCH PRACTICE

Test a diode safely with a multimeter

Use a standalone diode or LED and a digital multimeter with a diode-test function.

LOW-VOLTAGE ACTIVITY

Test procedure

  1. Disconnect the diode from any powered circuit. If it is installed in a circuit, isolate at least one lead when parallel paths could affect the result.
  2. Set the multimeter to diode-test mode.
  3. Place the red probe on the anode and black probe on the cathode.
  4. Record the displayed forward voltage if the meter can drive the device.
  5. Reverse the probes. A conventional diode should normally show open circuit or an out-of-range indication.
  6. Compare your result with the component datasheet and the meter manual.

What should you observe?

  • A silicon diode commonly shows a forward reading in the broad region of 0.5–0.8 V, depending on current, temperature and device.
  • A Schottky diode often shows a lower forward reading.
  • An LED may show a higher forward voltage, and some meters may not provide enough test voltage to make it glow.
  • Open or unusual readings can indicate wrong probe orientation, a damaged part, or a measurement limitation.

Never use diode-test mode on a live circuit. Do not test unknown high-voltage assemblies or capacitors that may retain charge.
