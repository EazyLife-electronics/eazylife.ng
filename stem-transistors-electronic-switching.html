01 · THE TRANSISTOR

A controllable semiconductor device

A transistor uses a small input signal to control a larger current or voltage. It can operate as an amplifier in its active region or as an electronic switch when driven between suitable off and on conditions.

Switching · Amplification · Control
02 · BJT BASICS

Base, collector and emitter

A bipolar junction transistor has base, collector and emitter terminals. In a simplified NPN low-side switch, a base current helps control collector current. The base-emitter junction behaves approximately like a diode, but its voltage and current depend on device and operating conditions.

03 · MOSFET BASICS

Gate, drain and source

A MOSFET is controlled primarily by gate-to-source voltage. A logic-level N-channel MOSFET may be suitable for low-side switching from a microcontroller, but check RDS(on) at the actual gate voltage, gate charge, current, thermal limits and safe operating area.

04 · SWITCHING CIRCUITS

Low-side control

A low-side NPN or N-channel MOSFET switch is placed between the load and ground. The load connects to the positive supply. The controller and load supply need a suitable common reference unless an isolation interface is used. Never exceed GPIO or transistor ratings.

05 · BIASING AND AMPLIFICATION

Operating point matters

In an amplifier, a transistor is biased to a suitable operating point so that the input signal can vary without unwanted clipping. BJT base resistors, emitter resistors, MOSFET gate networks and feedback influence operating conditions. Switching calculations are not a substitute for amplifier design.

06 · RELAYS AND MOTORS

Protect the control device

Relay coils and brushed DC motors are inductive loads. A suitable flyback diode or other suppression network is commonly needed for a DC coil. Check polarity, coil voltage, contact ratings, motor stall current and switching-device dissipation.

07 · COMPONENT SELECTION

Read the datasheet

Check maximum voltage, continuous and pulsed current, power dissipation, thermal resistance, gain or RDS(on), gate/base drive requirements, switching speed and safe operating area. Absolute maximum ratings are limits, not normal design targets.

08 · LOW-VOLTAGE PRACTICAL

Switch an LED or small DC load

Begin with a current-limited LED circuit or a small rated DC load. Use a suitable base/gate resistor, verify pinout from the exact datasheet and measure voltage across the switch. Add inductive-load suppression only with a correctly rated, correctly oriented component.

09 · COMMON ERRORS

Pinouts and drive levels are not universal

Transistor pin order varies between part numbers and packages. A MOSFET gate threshold voltage does not guarantee low on-resistance. A microcontroller pin cannot directly power a motor or relay coil. Heat sinking and transient protection may be essential.
