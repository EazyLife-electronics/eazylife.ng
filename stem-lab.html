Discover how voltage, resistance and current work together.

What is happening?

The resistor limits the current flowing through the LED. Lower resistance allows more current to flow.

Model note: simplified LED with a constant 2.0 V forward drop. Real LEDs have current and thermal limits.
EXPERIMENT 02 / ROBOTICS

Control a DC motor

Use PWM to change the average power delivered to a motor.

5 MIN
MOTOR SIMULATORRUNNING
DC MOTOR · PWM DRIVE
Duty cycle60 %
Estimated speed1,800 RPM
Average voltage7.2 V
Experiment controls
Idealised no-load motor model
What is happening?

PWM switches power rapidly on and off. At 60% duty cycle, the signal is on for 60% of each cycle, producing an average voltage of 7.2 V from a 12 V supply.

Model note: speed is estimated as proportional to average voltage. Real motors also depend on load, torque, friction and driver losses.
EXPERIMENT 03 / SENSORS

Measure distance with sound

See how an ultrasonic sensor measures the time taken by an echo.

5 MIN
HC-SR04 SIMULATORREADY
Measured distance30.0 cm
Echo time1,749 µs
Sound speed343 m/s
Move the object
Typical HC-SR04 range: 2–400 cm

Ready to measure. Send an ultrasonic pulse.

What is happening?

Distance = (echo time × speed of sound) ÷ 2. The division by two accounts for the sound travelling to the object and back again.

Model note: assumes a sound speed of 343 m/s at approximately 20°C and a clean echo.
