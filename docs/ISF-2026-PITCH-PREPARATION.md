# EazyLife — ISF Forum 2026 Pitch Preparation

**Event:** ISF Forum 2026 Pitch Battle  
**Date:** Saturday, 28 November 2026  
**Location:** ICC, Ibadan, Nigeria  
**Company:** EazyLife Robotics & Electronics Solutions  
**Website:** https://eazylife.ng  
**Founder:** Israel Ajayeoba

## Core positioning

EazyLife is an early-stage Nigerian engineering and technology company focused on robotics, electronics, automation, prototyping and affordable practical STEM.

Our larger ambition is to help Nigeria build the local engineering capacity to understand, design, adapt, prototype and progressively produce technology. STEM is an enabling pillar—not the company's final destination.

**Central message:**  
> We don't just want to be customers of the world's technology. We want to become creators, developers and producers of technology ourselves.

## Five-minute speaking script

### Slide 1 — Opening | 0:00–0:35

What if the next technology product we import into Nigeria could actually have been designed, developed or manufactured here?

And what if more people across our communities had the tools, knowledge and opportunity to turn their ideas into useful products?

That is the future EazyLife is working towards. We are an early-stage Nigerian engineering and technology company focused on robotics, electronics, automation, prototyping and affordable practical STEM. Our bigger ambition is to help build the local engineering capacity that makes home-grown products possible.

### Slide 2 — The problem | 0:35–1:10

Nigeria has a growing demand for technology, but we still depend heavily on finished products designed and manufactured elsewhere. That can make solutions expensive, difficult to adapt, and sometimes difficult to repair.

At the same time, people with ideas often struggle to access suitable components, tools and practical technical support. A student may have the curiosity and ability to become an engineer, but the cost of parts and training can shut that door before they get started.

The gap is not only access to products. It is access to the ability to understand, develop and improve them.

### Slide 3 — Our solution | 1:10–1:45

EazyLife brings four connected capabilities together: robotics and automation, electronics and hardware, affordable practical STEM, and prototyping with a pathway towards production.

These are not separate activities. Practical learning helps people understand technology. Access to components helps them experiment. Engineering and prototyping help turn ideas into working designs. Over time, that creates the foundation for products that can be developed and produced locally.

We will combine locally available materials and skills with imported components where necessary. The goal is not to avoid imports completely; it is to grow our own design, engineering, adaptation and production capability.

### Slide 4 — What exists today | 1:45–2:20

We have started by building our digital foundation. EazyLife is online, and our STEM Lab currently includes 20 interactive projects. One example is an ultrasonic servo radar experiment that introduces robotics and sensing concepts in an accessible way.

These are real digital assets that demonstrate our direction and our ability to build. We are still at an early stage. We do not yet have a dedicated engineering workshop or established small-scale manufacturing capacity. That is an important part of what we want to build next.

### Slide 5 — Customers and delivery | 2:20–2:45

Our customers include students and makers, schools, engineers, researchers and small businesses. Some need components or learning support. Others need help solving a practical engineering problem or developing a prototype.

We start by understanding the need, then source, teach, prototype or develop an appropriate solution. We use feedback to improve the work and identify opportunities for products that can serve more people.

### Slide 6 — Business model | 2:45–3:10

Our business model is designed around four revenue paths: electronics and robotics products and components; engineering services such as prototyping and integration; paid practical STEM programmes; and, as our product-development capacity grows, sales of EazyLife-designed products.

Services and component sourcing can help us understand customer needs and build relationships, while our longer-term opportunity is to develop repeatable products with greater local value.

### Slide 7 — Why EazyLife | 3:10–3:35

What makes EazyLife different is the connection we are building. A component supplier may provide the part. A learning platform may explain the theory. A workshop may build a one-off solution. We want to connect these stages so that people can move from accessing technology, to understanding it, to designing and developing practical solutions.

For us, STEM is important—but it is a supporting pillar. The bigger goal is engineering capability and locally developed technology.

### Slide 8 — The $10,000 opportunity | 3:35–4:25

The $10,000 prize would help us take our next major step: establishing our first dedicated EazyLife engineering workshop.

Our plan allocates $2,500 to workshop rent and setup, $2,000 to engineering tools and equipment, $1,500 to components and materials, and $1,500 to product development and testing. The remaining $2,500 supports the digital platform and STEM Lab, marketing, quality assurance, operations and working capital.

Our first priority is to secure and prepare the workspace, then install the tools and begin structured prototype development. This funding would help turn our engineering direction into a place where ideas can be designed, built, tested and refined.

### Slide 9 — Where we are going | 4:25–4:45

By 2029, our targets are to commercialise at least five locally designed products, serve 2,000 paying customers, build 20 partnerships and grow a team of 8 to 12 people. These are targets—not claims about where we are today.

We want to grow from a dedicated prototyping workshop into small-scale production, prove our products in Nigeria, and build the partnerships needed to reach wider markets.

### Slide 10 — Closing | 4:45–5:00

We are looking for capital, manufacturing partners, technical mentors, distributors and schools ready to build with us.

I am not asking you to invest in a company that has already arrived. I am asking you to believe in what we can build—and to help us build it.

We don't just want to be customers of the world's technology. We want to become creators, developers and producers of technology ourselves. Thank you.

## Optional founder observation: the ESP32 incident

A student was ready to buy a replacement ESP32 board because he believed his board was faulty. After asking a few questions, it became clear that the board had not been properly seated in the breadboard. The issue was not a damaged component; it was a practical hardware understanding gap.

Use this briefly if there is time or if a judge asks why practical engineering support matters. Do not present it as criticism of all training providers.

## Delivery reminders

- Pause after the opening question.
- Speak with conviction, not frustration, when describing import dependence.
- Make the four capabilities feel like one connected engineering pathway.
- Be precise about what exists now: the online platform and 20-project STEM Lab.
- Do not imply the workshop, manufacturing line or physical product portfolio already exists.
- Slow down on the $10,000 allocation.
- State clearly that the 2029 numbers are targets.
- Finish the closing statement slowly and make eye contact.

## Three-minute cut-down

If the pitch is shortened, prioritise slides **1, 2, 3, 4, 8 and 10**. Briefly cover the opening, the problem, the connected solution, current evidence, the use of the prize and the closing. Condense or skip slides 5, 6, 7 and 9, and leave out the optional ESP32 anecdote.
