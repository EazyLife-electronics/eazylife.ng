01 · FROM KNOWLEDGE TO A WORKING SYSTEM

Build to answer a real question

A project is more than connecting components until something moves or lights up. It is an opportunity to demonstrate that you can define a problem, select suitable parts, make the system behave as intended and collect evidence that it works under stated conditions.

01

Define

Describe the user, need, operating environment and measurable outcome.

02

Design

Draw the block diagram, choose interfaces and check component ratings.

03

Build

Prove risky subsystems first, then integrate wiring, firmware and enclosure.

04

Verify

Test normal operation, edge cases and faults. Record results and revisions.

Start smaller than your ambition. A reliable first version with clear test evidence is more valuable than a complicated prototype whose behaviour you cannot explain.

02 · CHOOSE A PROJECT

Project briefs you can develop

Choose according to the skills you want to practise and the tools available. The briefs are starting points, not fixed recipes. Adapt the requirements to your own context.

FOUNDATION + EMBEDDED

Smart room environment monitor

Build a device that measures room temperature, light level and humidity, then presents clear local readings.

Skills to demonstrate

  • Sensor interfacing and signal conditioning
  • Microcontroller ADC and digital communication
  • Display updates and data validation

Suggested system blocks

  • Temperature/humidity sensor and light sensor
  • Microcontroller with compatible logic levels
  • Display, protected supply and enclosure

Build milestones

  1. Read and validate each sensor independently
  2. Combine readings and handle missing data
  3. Add a clear display and enclosure

Evidence to collect

  • Comparison against a reference thermometer
  • Readings in bright and dim conditions
  • Behaviour when a sensor is disconnected

Extension challenge: Add min/max logging and a simple trend indicator without making the display difficult to read.

SENSING · INTERFACES

Smart room environment monitor

Combine light, temperature and humidity readings into a useful local instrument. Focus on sensor calibration, readable presentation and handling invalid data.

PRODUCT DESIGN · SENSING

Tank-level monitor

Measure tank level and show low, medium and high states. Design mounting, sensor range, fault indication and service access. Begin with indication only.

ROBOTICS · AUTONOMY

Line-following and obstacle-aware rover

Integrate reflectance sensors, a distance sensor, motor driver and state-based behaviour on a differential-drive chassis.

MOTOR CONTROL · FEEDBACK

Closed-loop DC motor controller

Measure shaft speed with an encoder, compare it with a target and use controlled PWM to reduce speed error under changing load.

EMBEDDED · NETWORKING

Local IoT monitoring dashboard

Send sensor telemetry from a microcontroller to a local web interface, with timestamps, stale-data handling and clear connection status.

INSTRUMENTATION · PRODUCT DESIGN

Programmable low-voltage test station

Create a protected test fixture that measures a selected low-voltage input, displays readings and guides repeatable component checks.

03 · PLAN THE BUILD

Use the same engineering discipline on every project

StageWork to completeEvidence before moving on
Problem and requirementsDefine who needs it, what it must do, operating limits, cost and safety boundaries.A short design brief with measurable acceptance criteria.
ArchitectureDraw sensor, controller, power, driver, communication and user-interface blocks.Block diagram and interface list with voltage/current assumptions.
Parts and calculationsCheck datasheets, pinouts, supply needs, resistor values, thermal limits and mechanical fit.Bill of materials with exact part variants and alternatives.
Subsystem testsTest sensors, drivers, displays, power and communication separately.Measurements and notes for each subsystem.
IntegrationCombine proven blocks, organise wiring and implement clear firmware states.Repeatable end-to-end demonstration.
VerificationTest expected values, boundary conditions, startup, reset and safe fault behaviour.Results against pass/fail criteria, including failures and corrections.
Revision and handoverImprove the design, document versions and explain how to operate and maintain it.Schematic, code, photos, test log and known limitations.

Safety boundary: These project directions are for appropriately designed extra-low-voltage learning prototypes. Do not turn a lesson into an exposed mains, high-energy battery, high-voltage or safety-critical build. Use current-limited supplies, rated drivers and independent protection where a physical hazard exists. A hobby sensor or prototype is not a certified life-safety device.

04 · PROJECT COMPLETION CHECK

Can another person understand and repeat your work?

Use this checklist before presenting a project as complete. Completion means more than a successful demonstration: it means you can explain the design and support your claims with evidence.

0 of 8 evidence checks complete

Build a portfolio, not just a collection

For each project, keep a short problem statement, block diagram, schematic, source code, photographs, measured results and a note about what you changed after testing. A few well-documented projects show more than many unexplained demonstrations.

Explain your decisions

Be ready to answer: Why this sensor? Why this driver? What happens when a reading is wrong? How did you choose the power supply? What did your measurements reveal? What would you change in a second revision?
