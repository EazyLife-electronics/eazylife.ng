01 · THE THREE QUANTITIES

What is happening in a circuit?

These quantities describe different aspects of electrical behaviour. They are related, but they are not interchangeable.

FOUNDATION
V

Voltage · volts (V)

Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points. It describes the energy transferred per unit of charge between those points.

Measured in volts · Symbol: V
I

Current · amperes (A)

Current is the rate at which electric charge passes a point in a circuit. One ampere is one coulomb of charge per second.

Measured in amperes · Symbol: I
R

Resistance · ohms (Ω)

Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes current. Its effect depends on the component, its operating condition and the applied voltage.

Measured in ohms · Symbol: R

A useful mental model: voltage is like a difference in electrical potential, current is the rate of charge flow, and resistance describes opposition to that flow. Water-flow comparisons can help beginners, but wires are not pipes and electrical behaviour has effects that the analogy cannot represent.

04 · MEASURE WITH A MULTIMETER

Connect the meter the right way

The measurement method changes depending on whether you are measuring voltage, current or resistance.

Measure voltage · in parallel

Place the probes across the two points whose potential difference you want to measure. The circuit normally remains connected and powered.

  • Black lead in COM.
  • Red lead in the V/Ω socket.
  • Select DC volts for a battery-powered DC circuit.
  • Start on a suitable range if the meter is not auto-ranging.
Voltage measurement: meter across the component or source.

Measure current · in series

Open the circuit at the measurement point and insert the meter so the current passes through it. Current measurement requires the correct current input and range.

  • Black lead in COM.
  • Red lead in the correct A or mA socket.
  • Begin with the highest suitable current range.
  • Break the circuit and place the meter in series.
Never place a meter in current mode directly across a battery or supply. This can create a short circuit and damage the meter, leads or source.

Measure resistance · power off

Resistance is measured on an unpowered circuit. Stored energy in capacitors must be safely discharged before testing.

  • Switch off and disconnect the supply.
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised.
  • Use COM and V/Ω sockets.
  • For reliable readings, isolate one resistor lead when parallel paths affect the result.
Resistance measurement: never test a live circuit in Ω mode.

Before every measurement: check the meter's socket, selector position, expected quantity, maximum rating and probe condition. Follow the meter manufacturer's manual. Never assume a meter's current input is fused or protected for every fault condition.

05 · BATTERIES IN SERIES AND PARALLEL

What changes when cells are combined?

Battery connections can change voltage or available capacity, but only within the limits of the cells and protection system.

+−

Series connection

Connect the positive terminal of one cell to the negative terminal of the next. Cell voltages add when the cells are correctly matched and connected in series.

ExampleTwo 1.5 V cells in series provide 3.0 V nominal.
+−

Parallel connection

Connect positive terminals together and negative terminals together. Voltage remains that of one cell; usable capacity may increase when cells are compatible and the system is designed for parallel operation.

ExampleTwo compatible 1.5 V, 2,000 mAh cells in parallel remain 1.5 V nominal, with a theoretical combined capacity of 4,000 mAh.
Battery safety matters.

Never casually parallel cells with different chemistry, model, age, state of charge or condition. Lithium cells require suitable protection, charging and current management. A pack's safe current is not determined by capacity (mAh) alone. Do not short battery terminals or mix cells in a pack.

06 · PRACTICAL LOW-VOLTAGE BUILD

Build and measure a resistor circuit

Use a current-limited, low-voltage DC supply and compare calculated values with real measurements.

WORKBENCH ACTIVITY

Components and tools

  • One regulated 5 V DC supply or suitable USB-powered source.
  • One 1 kΩ resistor, preferably ¼ W or higher.
  • One 2.2 kΩ resistor, preferably ¼ W or higher.
  • Breadboard and jumper wires.
  • Digital multimeter with intact probes.
  • Optional: two 1 kΩ resistors for the parallel comparison.

Use a current-limited bench supply where available. Do not connect this activity to mains electricity.

Investigation steps

  1. With power disconnected, place the 1 kΩ resistor in series with the 2.2 kΩ resistor.
  2. Check the wiring and calculate the total resistance: 3.2 kΩ.
  3. Connect the low-voltage supply only after checking for shorts and correct connections.
  4. Measure supply voltage across the series network.
  5. To measure current, switch off, open the circuit and insert the meter in series using the correct current socket and range.
  6. Measure each resistor's voltage drop in parallel, then compare the readings with the expected voltage division.
  7. Power off before changing the circuit to a parallel arrangement.

Expected ideal values at 5 V

Total resistance3.2 kΩ
Series current1.56 mA
Across 1 kΩ1.56 V
Across 2.2 kΩ3.44 V

Actual readings will vary with resistor tolerance, supply accuracy, meter accuracy and connection resistance. Use the measurements to investigate differences rather than treating small deviations as faults.

07 · THINK LIKE A TECHNICIAN

Troubleshoot the circuit logically

Start with the symptom, then test one possible cause at a time.

01

Current is zero

Look for an open connection, a disconnected supply, a component lead in the wrong breadboard row, or a meter that is not inserted in series.

02

Current is higher than expected

Check for a resistor value that is too low, an unintended parallel path, a wiring short or an incorrect supply voltage. Switch off before changing connections.

03

Voltage across a component is unexpected

Verify the measurement points, resistor values, supply voltage and whether other components or loads are connected to the same nodes.

04

Resistance reading keeps changing

Disconnect power, discharge capacitors safely and consider whether the resistor is still connected to other components that create parallel paths.
